# First-edition research record

Prepared 16 November 2026 (Singapore date), with AI research/drafting assistance. This is not a record of independent human peer review. Public bibliography and exact article-level locators are generated from Markdown frontmatter.

## Sources and access scope

- MacTutor biographies/topic essays (University of St Andrews) provide orientation and qualified historical context.
- Walter Eugene Clark's 1930 Āryabhaṭīya translation, including its preface's textual limitations, was consulted through an online transcription.
- GRETIL's Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta text, based on S. Dvivedin's edition, provides chapter/verse locators. Variant numbering is noted where relevant.
- Colebrooke's 1817 arithmetic/algebra translations were read via Internet Archive text. Original rules, commentator explanations, and translator interpretation are separate evidence. Future verbatim quotation should be checked against scans for OCR errors.
- Dutta's kuṭṭaka study and Kak's computational study support algorithmic/textual context.
- Sridharan's prosody chapter and Montelle's combinatorics research distinguish early text from later explanations and different counting problems.
- Ramasubramanian–Srinivas' *Development of Calculus in India* supports Kerala texts, series and rationales, with later witnesses distinguished from original works.
- Springer public contents and IIT Bhubaneswar's catalogue were consulted for the 2008 Gaṇita-Yukti-Bhāṣā edition. These support only visible publication/contents information and approximate c.1530 dating, **not** access to the paid full translation. The catalogue and scholarly survey support the adopted date; MacTutor contains inconsistent dates for this work.
- Ramanujan's 1919 partition paper §§1–2 and Hardy–Ramanujan's 1918 paper §1.1 were consulted via online collected-paper transcriptions. The Royal Society supports the fellowship chronology.
- Folkerts' manuscript-based calculation history and Allard's public abstract support the discussion of lost Arabic arithmetic and surviving Latin adaptations.

URLs, actual titles, and locators are in each article. The bibliography deduplicates by URL without discarding per-article locators. No externally sourced images appear in this edition.

## Deliberate limits

Early text dates and Āryabhaṭa's birthplace are qualified. Mādhava's attribution through later texts is explicit. The Līlāvatī daughter/wedding story is not treated as documented biography. Brahmagupta's area formula states the cyclic condition and zero arithmetic retains historical limitations. Piṅgala is not credited with all later combinatorics or modern binary concepts. No causal transmission of Kerala calculus to Europe is claimed.

Bakhshālī dating and “oldest zero” claims are omitted. Research surfaced a 2024 Oxford redating record; outdated 2017 news is not treated as a settled finding.

Most worked examples are original modern explanations, clearly marked. Their arithmetic was recalculated and the interactive mathematics has independent tests. A valid modern derivation does not establish historical authorship or discovery method. This selective collection is not a complete history or a claim of a single national origin for all mathematics.
